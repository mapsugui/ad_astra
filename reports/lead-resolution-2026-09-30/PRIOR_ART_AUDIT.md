## TOI-224.01 / TIC 70797900

### Primary source records and identity

* The official [STScI/NASA TESS Data Alerts table](https://stdatu.stsci.edu/prepds/tess-data-alerts/index.html) contains the exact row `TIC 70797900 / TOI 224.01`, with sector 2 coverage and the comments “V-shaped; single transit; likely EB.”  This is an archive alert record, not a confirmation of later events.  The local TESS_TOI query (2026-09-27) gives the same exact TIC/TOI identity, `t0=2458365.843684` BJD_TDB, no supplied period in the alert row, and duration 1.9189212 h.
* [Ziegler et al. 2021, *SOAR TESS Survey II: The Impact of Stellar Companions on Planetary Populations*](https://www.ctio.noirlab.edu/~atokovin/papers/Ziegler2021.pdf) is primary speckle-survey work.  It explicitly identifies TOI-224/TIC 70797900 as a close M-dwarf system with a companion at a projected separation of about 4 AU and calls it a likely eclipsing binary.  The paper does not publish an orbital RV solution or match a published event time to the Cygnus E1–E4 times.
* The earlier primary [SOAR TESS Survey I speckle data](https://www.ctio.noirlab.edu/~atokovin/papers/SOAR_TESS_speckle_imaging.pdf) has the exact TIC 70797900 row, observation epoch 2019.534, and an approximately 0.061 arcsec companion.  The imaging epoch is evidence for multiplicity, not an event timestamp.
* As a supporting primary light-curve paper, [Günther et al. 2020, *Stellar Flares from the First TESS Data Release*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.00443) lists TIC 70797900 as a single-transit candidate with a V-shaped event and notes that an eclipsing-binary interpretation is plausible.  It supplies no later event train or companion orbit.

### Exact-source archive evidence and limits

The refresh in [`archive_refresh.json`](archive_refresh.json) queried Gaia DR3 source `2320703325475764864` at the Gaia TAP endpoint with the exact source-ID selection on 2026-09-30.  The returned row is RA 1.9779893525, Dec -29.9796229916, RUWE 9.183665, radial velocity `5.0587792 +/- 4.4634724 km/s`, 21 accepted RV transits in 15 visibility periods, and `rv_amplitude_robust=49.801125 km/s`.  The exact `gaiadr3.nss_two_body_orbit` query returned zero rows.  The large robust amplitude is binary-compatible evidence, but without a published orbit, epoch-by-epoch velocities, source deblending, or a component assignment it is not a solved binary orbit.  RUWE is also sufficiently poor that astrometric/photometric priors remain suspect.

The existing NEA `pscomppars`/`TESS_TOI` cone query (30 arcsec, retrieved 2026-09-27) found no confirmed-planet row and only the exact TOI alert row; it did not establish a match to any Cygnus event time.  The selected MAST/SPOC search recorded S2, S29, S69, S96, and S106 products but did not exhaust all FFI/HLSP products, later releases, or all possible observing sectors.  No archival component-resolved RV time series was analyzed.  “No match” therefore means no match in these queried releases and services, not that the target has no additional observations.

**Conclusion:** prior art independently supports a close companion and an eclipsing-binary interpretation of the original single-sector alert, while the Cygnus event train remains unresolved.  E1–E3 being near the target does not overcome the companion/EB alternative, and E4 is a failed localization.  The lead should remain Unverified pending component-resolved photometry and phase-spread spectroscopy.

## TOI-2666.01 / TIC 170889511 / HD 80133 / HIP 45621

### Primary source records and identity

* [Zhang et al. 2024, AJ 167, 89, DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/ad1189](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ad1189) ([primary accepted PDF](https://research.usq.edu.au/download/0384f34d0aade96bac745a4563000aa6a40bffae5ddd07e64ee84cc9d2cd6f18/7870190/Zhang_2024_AJ_167_89.pdf)) identifies the exact Appendix A target as “HIP 45621 (TOI-2666).”  It states that TOI-2666.01 was a single transit in that work and that Keck/HIRES spectra show the star is a spectroscopic binary, with the secondary component separated by 35 km/s and a stated brightness relation.  That is direct primary spectroscopic evidence for binarity, but it is not a period solution for the current Cygnus aliases.
* [Absil et al. 2021, A&A 651 A45, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202140561](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202140561) ([primary PDF](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2021/07/aa40561-21.pdf)) independently resolves a point-like companion to HD 80133 with separation `6.0 +/- 0.2 mas`, position angle `13.2 +/- 0.7 deg`, and contrast `85.2 +/- 2.6%` in its Table 2.  Its discussion describes the system as a close pair and notes that an earlier RV survey had not identified it as a binary, plausibly because of incomplete coverage or an unfavorable orientation.  That old non-detection is a coverage limitation, not evidence against the resolved companion.
* The local exact-source identity audit maps the target to Gaia DR3 source `3837451574150437120` and SIMBAD `HD 80133`/`HIP 45621`, with the propagated Gaia position coincident at the target within the recorded matching tolerance.  This connects the literature names to the TESS TIC; it does not by itself add an event-time match.

### Exact-source archive evidence and limits

The 2026-09-30 Gaia refresh queried source `3837451574150437120`.  Gaia returned RUWE 1.464014 but null radial velocity, null RV uncertainty, null RV-transit count, and null robust RV amplitude; the exact `gaiadr3.nss_two_body_orbit` query returned zero rows.  Gaia’s missing RVS result does not contradict the Keck/HIRES or interferometric detections.  The local prior campaign also records no Gaia RVS velocity and an ESO TAP timeout on 2026-09-26; no multi-epoch archival RV sequence was analyzed.

The TESS_TOI record has no supplied period for TOI-2666.01.  The Cygnus reference event at BJD_TDB 2459259.15794 and E1 at 2461049.16442 are not times cited in either primary paper.  The Zhang “single transit” statement therefore cannot be used to reject the later E1, while the later E1 cannot be used to infer a binary period without the missing orbital/RV phase information.

**Conclusion:** the stellar-binary hypothesis is independently supported by two distinct primary observing methods, making a planet interpretation materially disfavored.  The current signal is nevertheless not resolved: the papers do not identify which component eclipsed, do not provide a phase-spanning SB2 orbit, and do not select among the 52 aliases.  Keep the Cygnus lead Unverified as an event interpretation; prioritize exact-source multi-epoch spectroscopy.

## TOI-3500.02 / TIC 443666343

### Primary source records and identity

* [Mugrauer, Rück & Michel 2023, *Gaia Search for Stellar Companions of TESS Objects of Interest IV*](https://doi.org/10.1002/asna.20230055) ([primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02452)) is a Gaia DR3 companion search.  Its exact Table 3/4 entries identify TOI-3500 A and B with Gaia astrometry: A parallax `5.1961 +/- 0.0275 mas`, B `5.2136 +/- 0.0277 mas`; the relative entry gives B separation `3.73911 +/- 0.00002 arcsec`, position angle `34.58936 +/- 0.00043 deg`, parallax difference `0.02 +/- 0.04 mas`, and relative proper motion `2.14 +/- 0.04 mas/yr`.  This is a high-significance common-proper-motion companion detection, not an RV orbit and not a TESS event attribution.
* The exact local Gaia identities are A=`3471495415361216512`, RA 186.816874744, Dec -29.833001532, G=11.346449, and B=`3471495419656596352`, RA 186.817554444, Dec -29.832146478, G=14.065311.  Their angular separation is about 3.74 arcsec, approximately 0.18 TESS pixel, which is consistent with the unresolved PRF degeneracy in the Cygnus reduction.

### Exact-source archive evidence and limits

The 2026-09-30 Gaia refresh queried both source IDs.  A has `RV=33.861237 +/- 0.2573572 km/s`, 20 accepted RV transits in 13 visibility periods, and robust amplitude `3.5316544 km/s`; B has `RV=35.688656 +/- 1.9666253 km/s`, 22 accepted RV transits in 14 visibility periods, and no robust amplitude.  The difference is not a resolved orbital measurement because the B uncertainty is large and the Gaia rows are blended-source catalog summaries.  The exact `gaiadr3.nss_two_body_orbit` query returned zero rows for both sources.  No paper or archive row found in this audit supplies component-resolved RV epochs tied to the TESS events.

The local TESS_TOI record gives TOI-3500.01 at `P=7.3436151 d`, `t0=2459315.357469` BJD_TDB and TOI-3500.02 with `t0=2460056.706283` and no period.  Cygnus S64/S90/S101 event times are not published in the Gaia companion paper.  The current reduction favors A in some Gaussian-PRF grids, but the preference changes under allowed registration/FWHM choices; S101/E2 is off-source and rejected.  The selected MAST query covered S64, S90, and S101 SPOC products, while FFI/HLSP/later-product completeness and archival RV coverage remain untested.

**Conclusion:** the existence of a co-moving stellar companion is independently resolved, but the source of E1 is not.  The companion’s sub-pixel separation makes source assignment the central unresolved issue; neither Gaia’s single catalog RV summaries nor the empty NSS table identifies the eclipsed component.  Keep the lead Unverified until calibrated PRF/WCS or resolved time-series photometry discriminates A from B.

## Cross-source coverage limits and reproducibility

The archive queries and retrieval timestamps are preserved in [`archive_refresh.json`](archive_refresh.json).  The earlier exact-source prior-art record (retrieved 2026-09-27) used NASA Exoplanet Archive `pscomppars` and `TESS_TOI` via `https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP/sync`, a 30 arcsec match radius, and exact TIC IDs; it found no confirmed-planet row for any of these TICs.  Gaia queries used `https://gea.esac.esa.int/tap-server/tap/sync`; the `gaiadr3.nss_two_body_orbit` selections above returned zero rows.  These nulls are release- and selection-bounded.  No claim here means “no observation exists” outside the named services, products, releases, epochs, and search radii.

The evidence resolves companion existence for all three systems to different strengths, and independently supports a stellar binary for TOI-2666, but it does not resolve any of the three Cygnus event interpretations.  The decisive follow-ups are source-localized photometry and phase-spread, component-aware spectroscopy; nominal local transit significance or a position-only catalog match cannot substitute for those tests.
